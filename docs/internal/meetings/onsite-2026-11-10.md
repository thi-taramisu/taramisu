## Sprint Retrospective

### Lessons Learned

Revisit previous agreements on timely reviews, assigned tickets, handovers,
and recording technical decisions in PRs.

Product owners want to stay involved in onsite meetings and hear what each person
did in the sprint, not only a high-level team summary. This onsite we spent more
time on per-person, detailed PR presentations with more people presenting, ran the
retro through retrotool.app, and did backlog cleanup (removed stale tasks and pinged
owners on outdated tests) before discussion. The extra time and structure matched
what they asked for; keep it.

| Observation | Agreed improvement | Owner | Check by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Retrospectives via retrotool.app (anonymous, five-minute timebox) were well received | Keep using retrotool for sprint retros | Team | Sprint #6 retro |
| POs want per-person sprint updates at onsite, not only aggregate outcomes | Keep longer PR presentations: each person presents what they did | Team | Sprint #6 onsite |
| Backlog cleanup before discussion cleared stale items and surfaced test follow-ups | Run cleanup before discussion each onsite | Scrum Master | Next onsite |
| PRs are generally processed faster; a few exceptions remain | Focus review on the remaining slow PRs | Team | Sprint #6 |
| PR descriptions and review comments are often too long | Keep text focused; guide AI agents to shorter PR content | Team | Ongoing |
| Onsite discussion topics were hard to collect ahead of Tuesday | Friday SM opens onsite doc PR from template; topics via PR comments; finalize after meeting | Rotating SM | Next Friday |
| Discord bot was useful but too noisy; frontend asked for it back after it was off | Re-enable with tweaks: always notify on PRs; once- or twice-daily digest for new issues | TBD | Sprint #6 |
