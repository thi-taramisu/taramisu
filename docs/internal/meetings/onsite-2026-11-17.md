## Sprint Retrospective

### Lessons Learned

Reviews and development progress were viewed positively. Frontend/backend coordination had improved, but shared milestones were missing.
The retrospective focused on effective reviews, unblocking dependent work and keeping the backlog current.

| Observation | Agreed improvement / follow-up | Owner | Check by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reviews support progress, but review quality remains an improvement area | Use human review together with AI assistance; assess findings instead of forwarding them without checking | Reviewers | Ongoing |
| Approval comments are being written without submitting an actual review approval | Use GitHub's **Approve** action after a positive review, including when there are no findings | Reviewers | Ongoing |
| Frontend/backend coordination is improving, but shared milestones and goals are missing | Record sprint goals and group related work into epics/milestones with a visible outcome | Scrum Master and team | Next planning check |
| Old or outdated tickets make the backlog hard to follow | Close completed issues, correct stale descriptions and reorder the remaining work as PRs progress | Issue owners | Ongoing |
| Stacked PRs hold up work above them | Make dependencies visible and prioritize reviews of PRs that unblock others; decide how to mark those PRs | PR authors and reviewers | Next sprint review |
| A two-week retrospective cycle might suit the team better | Reconsider the cadence at the next retrospective before making a change | Team | Next retrospective |
